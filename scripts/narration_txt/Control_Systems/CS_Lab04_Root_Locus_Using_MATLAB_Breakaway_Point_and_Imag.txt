[1:12] SCENE 2: THE MATLAB CODE
Define the open-loop system with tf, using the denominator vector 1, 6, 8, 0. The command rlocus draws the locus. Add sgrid to overlay lines of constant damping ratio and constant natural frequency. The command rlocfind lets you click on any point of the locus, and returns the gain K and the closed-loop poles there.

To check by calculation, use roots on the polynomial s cubed plus 6 s squared plus 8 s plus 48, which has the pair on the imaginary axis. The breakaway candidates come from roots of the derivative of the polynomial s cubed plus 6 s squared plus 8 s. Remember, only the one on the locus is valid.

[2:54] LIVE DEMO: WORKED EXAMPLE AND EXPECTED RESULTS
Run the script. MATLAB draws three branches starting at 0, minus 2 and minus 4. Two branches meet on the real axis between minus 2 and 0, then break away. Use rlocfind and click at the breakaway point. You will read a gain of about 3.08, with the pole near minus 0.845. This matches our calculation of K equal to 3.08.

The derivative of the polynomial has two roots, minus 0.845 and minus 3.155. The second one lies on a segment that is not part of the locus, so it is not a breakaway point, as we explained in class.

Now click where the locus crosses the imaginary axis. You should get K close to 48, and poles at plus or minus j 2.83. Check with roots: the polynomial s cubed plus 6 s squared plus 8 s plus 48 gives minus 6, and plus or minus j 2.83. The system is stable for K from 0 to 48.

For design, suppose we want a damping ratio of 0.5. Click where the locus meets the 0.5 damping line from sgrid. The gain is about 8.3, and the dominant poles are at minus 0.667 plus or minus j 1.155, with the third pole far away at minus 4.67. With the third pole six times farther from the axis, the dominant pair controls the response, which justifies treating it as a second-order system.

Question 2. What do the asymptotes depend on? On the difference between the number of poles and zeros, and their centroid.

Question 3. What does the imaginary-axis crossing tell us? The gain beyond which the system is unstable, and the frequency of sustained oscillation.

[5:58] COMMON LAB MISTAKES AND VIVA TIPS
Four practical tips. One: when using rlocfind, click as close as you can to the locus. Use the zoom tool on the plot before clicking, otherwise the answer will have a small error. Two: write your hand-calculated values and MATLAB values side by side, and comment on any difference.

Three: do not accept a breakaway root just because the derivative gave it. Check that it lies on a segment of the real axis that belongs to the locus. Four: save the figure and label the axes. For the viva, be ready to explain how the number of branches, the asymptotes, and the centroid relate to the poles and zeros.
