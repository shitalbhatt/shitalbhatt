[0:37] SCENE 1: THE THREE SYSTEMS
We use three systems, all with unity feedback. The base system has the open-loop transfer function 4 over s into s plus 2. The second adds a pole at minus 10, and the gain is adjusted to 40 so that the low-frequency behaviour is the same. The third adds a zero at minus 1 to the base system. We compare their closed-loop poles and step responses.

[1:05] SCENE 2: THE MATLAB CODE
Define the base system G0 as tf of 4 over the vector 1, 2, 0. For the second system, multiply s into s plus 2 by s plus 10 using conv, with numerator 40. For the third, the numerator is the vector 4, 4, which is 4 s plus 4. Close the loops with the feedback command and unity feedback. Then plot all three on the same graph by passing them to the step command.

Use pole to display the closed-loop poles, stepinfo to read the specifications, and pzmap to see the poles and zeros on the s-plane.

The feedback command takes the open-loop system and the feedback path. For unity feedback, the second argument is simply one.

Let us write the lab sheet properly. The aim: to study the effect of adding poles and zeros to the open-loop transfer function on the closed-loop response and stability. You will need: a computer with MATLAB and the Control System Toolbox.

[2:47] LIVE DEMO: WORKED EXAMPLE AND EXPECTED RESULTS
Run the script. The base system has closed-loop poles at minus 1 plus or minus j 1.73. Zeta is 0.5 and omega n is 2. The overshoot is 16.3 percent and the settling time is about 4 seconds.

Now add the pole at minus 10. The poles become minus 10.45 and a complex pair at minus 0.774 plus or minus j 1.80. The complex pair has moved closer to the imaginary axis, so the damping is smaller. The overshoot increases to about 25 percent, and the settling time to 4.4 seconds. Lesson: adding a pole, which adds lag, reduces stability.

Now add the zero at minus 1. The closed-loop poles become minus 0.764 and minus 5.236. Both real, so no oscillation at all, and the overshoot is 0. The settling time drops to about 3.4 seconds. But be careful: the zero does not cancel anything here, it just changes where the poles land. In general, a zero in the left half-plane adds phase lead and tends to increase the speed.

Finally, stability against gain. With three poles, the characteristic equation is s cubed plus 12 s squared plus 20 s plus K. The Routh array shows stability for K below 240. At K equal to 240 the poles are at plus or minus j 4.47. At 300 the poles move into the right half-plane, and the step response is a growing oscillation. This is why adding poles limits the gain.

[5:03] VIVA QUESTIONS AND MODEL ANSWERS
Before you go to the viva, test yourself. Pause after each question and answer it aloud.

Question 1. What is the effect of adding a pole in the left half-plane to the open-loop transfer function? It adds phase lag, reduces relative stability, and increases the overshoot.

Question 2. And a zero in the left half-plane? It adds phase lead and usually makes the response faster, and can reduce overshoot.

Question 3. Why does the three-pole system become unstable at a high gain? Because the closed-loop poles move to the right and cross the imaginary axis at K equal to 240.

Question 4. How do you find the critical gain? From the Routh array, or from the root locus.

Question 5. Which MATLAB commands did you use? tf, conv, feedback, step, stepinfo, pole and pzmap.

[6:00] COMMON LAB MISTAKES AND VIVA TIPS
Four tips for your record. One: use conv to multiply polynomials like s into s plus 2 into s plus 10. Typing them by hand invites errors. Two: when comparing systems, keep the low-frequency gain comparable, otherwise you are comparing different systems.

Three: always inspect the closed-loop poles, not just the open-loop poles. Four: for each observation, write the reason. For example: adding a pole at minus 10 increased the overshoot because it added phase lag and pushed the dominant poles toward the imaginary axis. A viva question may be, what happens when you keep adding poles? The answer: the critical gain falls, and the system eventually becomes unstable.

[6:47] RECAP
Let us recap in five points. One: adding a pole adds lag, and usually reduces stability and increases the overshoot Two: adding a left half-plane zero adds phase lead and usually makes the response faster Three: the feedback command closes the loop Four: pzmap shows the poles and zeros on the s-plane Five: with more poles, the critical gain for stability becomes smaller
