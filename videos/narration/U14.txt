@video L27-28_Dimensionality_Reduction_Lecture
D:1 | Introduction | Welcome to lectures twenty seven and twenty eight: dimensionality reduction. P C A, L D A, t-S N E and U M A P, and V I F.
D:2 | Roadmap | Lecture twenty seven is about fewer, cleaner features: multicollinearity and V I F, the idea of P C A, variance explained, and interpreting components. Lecture twenty eight is about labels and pictures: L D A, the classification view, t-S N E and U M A P for pictures only, and which method when. Our data is Wine Quality: six thousand four hundred ninety seven wines, red and white, with eleven lab measurements.
D:3 | Multicollinearity and VIF | Multicollinearity is when one feature can be predicted from the others. The density of wine depends on its sugar and its alcohol, so density adds almost no new information. In regression, linked features make the weights unstable and hard to read. A correlation heat map only checks pairs; V I F checks each feature against all the others. V I F equals one over one minus R squared, where R squared says how well the other features predict this one.
D:4 | VIF on the wine data | On the wine data, density has a V I F of fifteen point nine, residual sugar seven point two, fixed acidity four point nine, and alcohol four point eight. The guide: one means no link, one to five is moderate and usually fine, five to ten is high, check it, and above ten is serious. Drop density and the largest V I F falls to two point eight. Or use P C A, whose components always have V I F equal to one.
D:5 | PCA: the idea | Principal Component Analysis rotates the data to new axes. P C one is the direction in which the data spreads the most. P C two is the next biggest spread, at ninety degrees to P C one. Keep a few, since the first few P Cs hold most of the information. Each P C is a weighted mix of all features, P Cs are uncorrelated, and P C A does not use the target.
D:6 | Always scale before PCA | Always scale before P C A. P C A chases the biggest variance. Unscaled, total sulfur dioxide, with a standard deviation of fifty seven, beats density, with a standard deviation of point oh oh three. P C one explains ninety five percent just because of units. After standard scaling, every column has standard deviation one, and P C one explains twenty seven point five percent.
D:7 | Variance explained | How many P Cs? The scree plot shows bars of percent explained and a line of cumulative percent. Three rules: the elbow of the scree plot suggests three; the Kaiser rule, eigenvalue above one, gives three; and reaching ninety percent of variance needs seven. P C one plus P C two hold fifty percent of the information in eleven features.
D:8 | Interpreting components | To interpret components, read the loadings. A loading is the weight of a feature in a P C, and you read the largest positive and negative weights. P C one has sulfur dioxide and sugar on one side and volatile acidity and chlorides on the other: white style versus red style. P C two has density against alcohol: heavy versus light. P C three has citric and fixed acidity against pH: acidity.
D:9 | Check the name | Check the name: P C A never saw the wine type. Yet red wines sit on the left, with an average P C one of minus two point five, and white wines on the right, with plus point eight. P C one really is a red versus white style axis.
D:10 | Reading a biplot | Reading a biplot has three rules. Arrows in the same direction mean the features rise together, like the two sulfur dioxides. Opposite directions mean one rises while the other falls, like density and alcohol. And points lie towards the features they are high in.
D:11 | LDA, t-SNE and UMAP | Lecture twenty eight: using labels to separate classes, and drawing pictures of clusters.
D:12 | LDA | L D A reduces dimensions to separate classes. P C A does not use class labels and finds directions of largest spread; L D A uses labels and finds directions of best class separation. L D A's maximum components is the number of classes minus one. P C A is used to compress and explain; L D A to classify. For example, quality grouped as low, medium and high is three classes, so L D A gives two components.
D:13 | LDA beats PCA at classification | L D A beats P C A at classification. Using the same two dimensions to predict quality group, two L D A components give fifty seven point seven percent test accuracy, two P C A components give forty eight point six, and always guessing medium gives forty three point six. L D A chose its dimensions to separate the classes.
D:14 | t-SNE and UMAP | t-S N E and U M A P are for pictures only. They keep neighbours close, giving clear clusters, as in two thousand red and white wines. But the axes have no meaning, gaps and sizes are not reliable, the picture changes with the random seed and settings, and t-S N E cannot place new rows.
D:15 | Which method when | Which method when? To find and fix linked features: V I F, then drop, or use P C A. To compress features and keep the meaning: P C A with loadings. To separate known classes or classify: L D A. To look at clusters in a picture: t-S N E or U M A P. To feed a model: P C A or L D A, never t-S N E.
D:16 | Key takeaways | Key takeaways. V I F above ten is serious multicollinearity; density scored fifteen point nine. For P C A, scale first; P C one plus P C two hold fifty percent of eleven features. Name each P C from its largest loadings. And L D A uses labels, while t-S N E and U M A P are for pictures only. Thanks for watching.

P:1 | Introduction | Welcome to practicals twenty seven and twenty eight: P C A in Python, with the wine quality and breast cancer datasets.
P:2 | Lab plan | Practical twenty seven implements P C A: load and scale, check V I F, fit P C A, variance explained, and how many P Cs. Practical twenty eight visualises and interprets: loadings heat map, P C scatter, loading plot, and a worked breast cancer example. We use wine quality, six thousand four hundred ninety seven rows, as the main example, and breast cancer, five sixty nine rows and thirty features, as the worked example.
P:3 | Step 1: load and scale | Step one: load the data, take the eleven lab measurements as features, and scale with Standard Scaler. P C A follows the largest variance. Total sulfur dioxide has a standard deviation of fifty six point five, density only point oh oh three. Without scaling, P C one would explain ninety five percent only because sulfur dioxide has the biggest numbers. After scaling every feature has standard deviation one.
P:4 | Step 2: VIF | Step two: check multicollinearity with V I F. It is the diagonal of the inverse correlation matrix, the same result as running eleven separate regressions. Density has fifteen point nine one, above ten, so serious. Residual sugar is seven point two three. Drop density and the largest V I F is two point eight four. P C A components always have V I F of one.
P:5 | Step 3: fit PCA | Step three: fit P C A, keeping all eleven components, and call fit transform to learn the axes and give each wine its P C values. The result is still six thousand four hundred ninety seven by eleven, because P C A only rotates the axes. The correlation between P Cs is zero, so there is no repeated information.
P:6 | Step 4: variance explained | Step four: variance explained. Eigenvalue is the variance carried by a P C; on scaled data the total is eleven, one per feature. P C one has eigenvalue three point oh three, twenty seven point five percent. P C two is twenty two point seven. Together they hold fifty percent, and seven P Cs reach ninety percent.
P:7 | Scree and cumulative plots | Plot the scree plot, one bar per P C, which shows an elbow after P C three, and the cumulative plot, which crosses the ninety percent line at seven components.
P:8 | Step 5: how many | Step five: how many components to keep? Eigenvalue above one gives three. Passing a number below one, like zero point nine, to n components means keep enough P Cs for ninety percent of the variance, giving seven. The elbow gives three for a quick look, Kaiser three for scaled data, and a ninety percent target seven to keep most information.
P:9 | Step 6: loadings | Step six: loadings show what each P C is made of. Draw them as a heat map. P C one is white style, sulfur dioxide and sugar, against red style, volatile acidity and chlorides. P C two is heavy, density, against light, alcohol. P C three is acidity, citric and fixed, against pH.
P:10 | Step 7: PC scatter | Step seven: plot each wine on P C one and P C two, coloured by type. The average position of red wines is minus two point five one on P C one, white wines plus point eight two. P C A never saw type, yet red and white separate along P C one, so the name red versus white style is right.
P:11 | Step 8: loading plot | Step eight: the loading plot shows how the features point. One quiver call draws one arrow per feature, all in one command. Same direction means features rise together, like the two sulfur dioxides. Opposite means one up, one down, like density and alcohol.
P:12 | Breast cancer example | The same code on the breast cancer data. The largest V I F is mean radius at three thousand eight hundred six. P C one and P C two explain forty four point three and nineteen percent. Seven of thirty P Cs reach ninety percent, and six have eigenvalue above one. Thirty features become two numbers that separate benign from malignant, with P C one meaning large, irregular shape, on the malignant side.
P:13 | What the lab showed | What the lab showed. Scale, P C A, fit transform: three lines of code. Wine: P C one plus P C two is fifty percent; three P Cs by Kaiser, seven for ninety percent. Loadings name the P Cs, and the P C scatter confirms the names. And on breast cancer, thirty features become two P Cs that separate the diagnoses. Thanks for watching.
