#1
Let me explain how the marks usually come. Examiners look for a correct sequence, correct code, and correct interpretation. A neat notebook with short comments scores better than a clever one that is hard to follow. And a wrong answer with a clear reason can still earn partial credit, whereas a blank cell earns nothing. So the plan is to make steady progress through a fixed routine, not to chase a perfect result in one part. Starting with the same ten steps every time gives you structure under pressure.
#2
Let me explain how the ten steps map to the course. Steps one to three are module one and module three basics, the audit. Steps four and five are the univariate work of module two. Step six is the bivariate and multivariate work. Steps seven and eight are module three and four, the cleaning and encoding. Step nine applies only if the question asks for it. Step ten is the part many students forget: write what the results mean in plain words, because that is where the interpretation marks are.
#3
Let me give you the five lines to type from memory. df equals pd dot read csv with the file name. df dot shape and df dot dtypes. df dot head. df dot isna dot sum. And df dot duplicated dot sum. After that, the transposed check, df dot T dot duplicated dot sum, finds repeated columns. If any of these produce a surprise, write a comment about it. These lines take under a minute, and they stop you from building on a broken table.
#4
Let me explain the one-line summaries. df dot describe gives a quick overview, and df dot skew and df dot kurt add the shape. For the interquartile range, take the difference of the quantiles at point seven five and point two five. The outlier count compares each value with the lower and upper fences. Then sns dot histplot and sns dot boxplot give the two pictures. Finally, write one sentence of interpretation for each column, for example that the mean is above the median, so the data is right-skewed.
#5
Let me remind you of the main commands. df dot corr gives correlation, and method equal to spearman gives the rank version. pd dot crosstab with normalize equal to index gives row percentages. df dot groupby with a column and dot agg gives grouped statistics. For the variance inflation factor, use the statsmodels function, once per column. And draw a heatmap of the correlation matrix, since a picture is worth more than a table in a short time. Write a sentence for each pair that you consider important.
#6
Let me remind you of the handling steps. For hidden missing values, replace zero or nine ninety nine with NaN in the right columns. For missing values, fillna with the median for skewed data, or use an imputer in a pipeline. For outliers, cap with the interquartile range fences, or log transform, and say why. For categories, get dummies with drop first, or an ordered map for an ordinal column. And for skewed columns, np dot log one p. If a model is needed, put the steps in a pipeline, and fit on the training rows only.
#7
Let me give you a time plan as an example. If you have two hours, use the first ten minutes to read the question and load the data. Spend twenty minutes on the audit and cleaning decisions. Spend thirty minutes on the exploration and plots. Thirty minutes on handling, encoding and any modelling. And twenty minutes on interpretation, on checking that the notebook runs from top to bottom, and on tidying. Adjust to your actual time. Common mistakes are skipping the audit, forgetting to explain, and leaving code that fails when the notebook is rerun.

Two rehearsals like this will make the real assessment feel familiar. Stay calm, work steadily, and good luck.
