Let me explain why postal code is dropped. It is stored as a number, and pandas will include it in numeric calculations, but averaging postal codes or correlating them with sales has no meaning, because the digits are labels. This is the data types lesson from earlier lectures in action. The way to avoid the trap is to look at the list of columns selected and ask for each one whether arithmetic on it makes sense. For categorical columns, avoid those with hundreds of levels, such as product name, because the tables become unreadable.

Let me add how to read this. Covariance of about seventy thousand between sales and profit is positive, so they rise together, but because the units are dollars squared, we cannot say whether seventy thousand is large. The discount and profit covariance of minus ten point six is small in size, but discount is a rate between zero and point eight, so a small number may still represent a strong relationship. This is exactly why we move to correlation, where every pair is on the same scale from minus one to plus one.

Let me add the interpretation. A correlation of about zero point five between sales and profit says that larger orders tend to be more profitable, but with plenty of exceptions. The weak link between sales and quantity is a little surprising, but it makes sense because unit prices vary so much. The jump in discount versus profit under Spearman tells you that the relationship is more consistent than Pearson suggests. Whenever the two coefficients differ a lot, one of the next steps should be a scatterplot to understand why.

Let me add how to read the plots. In the boxplot by category, compare the medians first, then the spread, then the dots. If all three medians are near zero while the means differ, the means are driven by outliers. In the bar plot of average sales by segment, remember that seaborn adds a small black line showing the uncertainty in the mean, called a confidence interval. If the lines of two bars overlap heavily, the difference between them may be due to chance.
