#0
Let me explain what this video is for. It is a map of the whole course, from first question to final insight, in twelve steps. If you ever feel lost in a project, come back here and ask which step you are at. It is also a good way to revise before an assessment, because each step points to the lectures and labs where you can find the details. Keep a pen ready, and write the twelve steps on a single sheet of paper as we go.
#1
Let me explain these four steps with a one-line reminder each. Framing is lecture one and the first lab. The audit is the five commands from module three, applied early. Data types come from lectures three and four, and their lab. Distributions come from lecture two and the univariate labs. The common thread is that you spend time understanding the table before drawing conclusions. Skipping these steps is the most common reason for results that look good but are wrong.
#2
Let me explain these four steps. Relationships and groups come from module two, with correlation, cross-tabs and clustering. Visualisation and storytelling come from lectures thirteen and fourteen, and they prepare you for the assignment presentation. Data quality is the heart of module three, covering integration, duplicates, encoding and rules. And missing values and outliers get a separate step each because they need their own reasoning. Notice that exploring and cleaning go back and forth, as you explore, you find issues to clean, and after cleaning, you explore again.
#3
Let me explain these last four steps. Encoding and imbalance come from lectures twenty-one and twenty-two. Transformation, scaling, feature engineering and selection come from module four. Dimension reduction uses PCA and its relatives, and testing uses stratified splits, cross-validation and leakage control. The final step is the one that matters to others: turning results into insights with observation, evidence, meaning and action, and stating the limits. If you can explain these twelve steps in your own words, you have understood the course.
#4
Let me turn the four ideas into questions you ask in every project. Have I looked at both the numbers and the picture? Do I know why this value is missing or extreme before I fix it? Is there any path by which the test data could have influenced a decision? And have I said clearly what is exploratory, what is confirmed, and what the data cannot tell us? If the answer to any of these is no, fix that first. These four questions are what professional reviewers check too.
