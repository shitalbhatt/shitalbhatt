Let me explain the five commands. df dot shape gives rows and columns. df dot isna dot sum dot sum gives the total number of missing cells. df dot duplicated dot sum gives the number of rows that exactly repeat an earlier row. The transpose trick, df dot T dot duplicated, treats each column as a row, so it finds columns that are exact copies of each other, such as price and price in dollars. Together these five lines take seconds, and they tell you more about the data than a long look at the first few rows.

Let me explain the code. Use df dot groupby of the two columns, then the price column, then dot transform with median, which returns a value for each row, equal to its group median. Divide the price by this peer median to get the ratio. Then sort the data frame by the ratio, and look at the top rows. Be careful when the peer median is zero or very small, as the ratio will explode, so check the group sizes too. The ten thousand dollar private room in Queens, against a peer median of fifty nine, has a ratio of about one hundred seventy.

#8
Let me explain how to calculate it. df dot isna dot mean times one hundred gives the percentage missing per column. For the row level, df dot isna dot any with axis equals one gives true for rows with at least one gap, and one minus its mean gives the share of fully complete rows. Notice that a column can have only twenty percent missing, but across seventeen columns this adds up, so only about seventy-nine percent of the rows are complete. If you drop every row with a gap, you lose twenty percent of your data, which is a reason to be careful.
#9
Let me explain how to build this cross-tab. Create a boolean column for reviews equal to zero, and another for last review is missing, then call pd dot crosstab on the two. If the table has only two filled cells, on the diagonal, then the two facts are perfectly linked. That is how we know the missing values have a structural reason. This pattern, comparing a missing indicator with another column, is the standard way to investigate why data is missing, and you will use it again in the lecture on missing values.
