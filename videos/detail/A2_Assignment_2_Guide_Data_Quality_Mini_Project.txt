Let me begin with the usual reminder. Your assignment brief decides the exact requirements, the dataset rules, the deadline and the submission format. Treat this video as advice on how to get a high mark from what you already learned in module three. The key difference from Assignment one is that now the marks reward what you changed and why, and not only what you found. So your notebook has to tell a story of before and after.
#1
Let me explain what evidence means in practice. For every claim such as I handled the missing values, a marker wants to see a table or a chart. How many were missing, which method you used, and what changed in the distribution afterwards. Without that, the statement is just words. Plan your notebook so that each cleaning step has three parts: the problem, the fix, and the proof. That structure also makes your work easy to read, and easy to mark.
#2
Let me give you a suggestion on how to use each stage. The audit gives the overview with shape, missing values, duplicates and types. The four dimensions checks give you your list of issues. Integration is only needed if your data comes from more than one source, and if it does not, say so. Missing values need a reason and a method. Outliers need a rule and a treatment. Encoding and imbalance apply if you have categorical features or a rare target. Validation closes the loop by re-running your rule table. Not every dataset needs every stage, but you should say which you skipped and why.
#3
Let me explain what a good log looks like. It is a table with one row per issue and columns for the issue, how many rows were affected, the suspected cause, the decision, and the effect. For example: ten thousand dollar price, three rows, probable typing error, capped at the upper fence, mean price fell from one fifty three to one twenty nine. Be sure to treat disguised missing values properly, such as zeros in a column where zero is impossible. A log like this can be reused in your final project, so it is worth doing well.
#4
Let me explain what to compare. For missing values, make a small table with mean, median, K nearest neighbours and regression imputation, and show the standard deviation retained by each. The method that keeps the spread closest to the original is usually preferable. For outliers, show the mean, standard deviation and skew before and after, and a model score before and after. For encoding, explain why one-hot is right for nominal columns and an ordered map for ordinal ones. Numbers like these show judgement.
#5
Let me explain the leakage warning with an example. If you fill missing values with the mean of the whole dataset before splitting, the test rows have contributed to that mean, and your test score is slightly optimistic. The correct order is to split first, learn the mean from the training rows, and apply it to both. The same goes for scalers, target encoders and any resampling. In scikit-learn, a pipeline makes this automatic, and it is worth learning for your final project. Also list the limitations: what your cleaning cannot fix, and what claims your cleaned data still cannot support.

And practise explaining each decision in one sentence, because you will probably be asked why you chose a particular method. Good luck, and I look forward to seeing your work.
