Let me explain how to do it. df dot select dtypes include number, then dot skew gives the skew for every numeric column. Choose those above, say, one in absolute value, excluding identifiers. Apply np dot log one p to each of them, store them in new columns with a suffix such as underscore log, and join them back. Why log one p and not log? Because log of zero is undefined, and log one p of zero is zero, so zero-dollar prices and zero reviews do not break the code. Remember that log only works for non-negative values.

Let me explain the order. If you scale first and take the log afterwards, you cannot, because scaled values can be negative. And if you scale before dealing with outliers, those outliers influence the mean and standard deviation, which then shift the scaled values of every other row. So the safe order is: fix errors, log or cap the long tails, and then scale. In scikit-learn, you create the scaler, call fit transform on the chosen columns, and put the results into new columns. Check by calling describe that the means are close to zero and standard deviations close to one.
