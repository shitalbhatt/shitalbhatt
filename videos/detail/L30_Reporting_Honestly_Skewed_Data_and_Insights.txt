#1
Let me explain this with the numbers. If a shop receives ten thousand visits and fifteen hundred sixty buy, then a model that always predicts no is right on eight thousand four hundred forty visits, which is eighty-four percent, but it misses every single buyer, so it has zero recall. In medical screening, a model that always says healthy could look very accurate and miss every patient. Recall, which is the share of true buyers that you found, and F one, which balances recall and precision, tell the real story. Always print the always-no baseline next to your model.
#2
Let me explain why this matters. If you want to look at a sample of the data to understand it, a simple random sample can by chance under-represent or over-represent the rare group, as the eighteen percent shows. A stratified sample guarantees the same share. In pandas, df dot groupby of the target dot sample with frac equal to point oh two does it. In scikit-learn, train test split with stratify equal to y does the same for splits. Whenever a class is rare, stratify, so the rare group is neither missing nor overrepresented in any part.
#3
Let me explain what each option does. Class weights tell the model that a mistake on a buyer costs more than a mistake on a non-buyer, so it adjusts its decision boundary. Under-sampling throws away many non-buyers so the classes are balanced, which is fast but wastes data. Over-sampling copies buyers, or makes synthetic ones with SMOTE, which keeps all the data but can over-fit to repeated rows. The trade-off is clear here: a small drop in accuracy for a large gain in recall. Whether that is worthwhile depends on the business cost of missing a buyer compared with a false alarm.
#4
Let me explain the four parts with one example. Observation: pages with value drive buying. Evidence: fifty-six percent versus four percent. Meaning: visitors who reach those pages already intend to buy. Action: guide visitors toward product pages. Compare this with a weak statement, such as the correlation between page value and revenue is point four. The strong version tells a manager what to do. Also remember the honesty rules from earlier lectures: label what was exploratory, show uncertainty, and do not claim cause from correlation. An insight that cannot be acted on, or that overclaims, is not yet an insight.
