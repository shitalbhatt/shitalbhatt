#6
Let me explain the coefficient of variation. It is the standard deviation divided by the mean, and it expresses spread as a fraction of the average, so you can compare features with different units, such as dollars and units. A larger value means more relative variation. It only makes sense for ratio data with a positive mean, so do not use it for profit, which can be negative. The outlier count uses a boolean comparison: values below the lower fence or above the upper fence, then sum to count the trues.

Let me explain how to interpret an outlier percentage. Twenty percent for profit does not mean that a fifth of the orders are errors. It means the distribution is very wide compared with its middle half. Large gains and large losses are both genuine business events, so we keep them. But in modelling, we would think of a robust method, or a transformation, or splitting by category. The interpretation sentence in your notebook should say what the outliers are, whether they are errors, and what you decided to do.

Let me show the one-liner. In pandas you can write df of profit less than zero, then dot mean, which gives the proportion of true values, and multiply by one hundred to get a percentage. It works because true counts as one and false as zero. This pattern of taking the mean of a condition is one of the most useful tricks in data analysis, and you can use it for any yes or no question, for example what share of orders have a discount above ten percent.
