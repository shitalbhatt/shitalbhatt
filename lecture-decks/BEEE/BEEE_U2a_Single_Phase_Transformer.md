| 3 | Recap: What You Already Know | • Faraday: e = −N·dΦ/dt.<br>• Changing flux induces EMF in a coil.<br>• Sinusoidal flux gives sinusoidal EMF.<br>• Mutual induction: flux linking two coils.<br>**Formula/Key:** e = −N·dΦ/dt;   RMS = peak/√2 | Flux and induced EMF waveforms with the 90° lag marked (preview of the EMF equation). | Faraday's law says that the EMF induced in a coil equals the number of turns times the rate of change of flux. For a sinusoidal flux the EMF is sinusoidal and lags the flux by ninety degrees. If two coils share the same changing flux, an EMF appears in each one. That is mutual induction, and it is the principle of the transformer. |
| 4 | Motivation: The Phone Charger to the Power Grid | • Charger: 230 V AC → 5 V.<br>• Generators produce about 11 kV.<br>• Transmission at 132 kV and above.<br>• Distribution at 11 kV, then 415/230 V.<br>**Formula/Key:** Transformers connect all the voltage levels of the grid | Block chain from generator to load with step-up and step-down transformers and voltage levels. | Every power chain has several transformers: a step-up near the generator, step-down transformers near the load, and a tiny one inside the phone charger. They allow each part of the system to work at the most suitable voltage. Without transformers, long-distance AC transmission would be impractical. |
| 5 | Necessity of Transformers | • Power P = V·I stays the same.<br>• Higher voltage means lower current.<br>• Line loss is I²R: it falls sharply.<br>• Smaller conductors, higher efficiency.<br>• Safe voltage at the consumer end.<br>**Formula/Key:** Ploss = I²R = (P/V)²R   →   10× voltage gives 1/100 loss | Same chain with a callout showing that a ten-times higher voltage cuts line loss by 100. | For a given power the current is inversely proportional to the voltage. Since the loss in the line is I squared R, raising the voltage ten times reduces the loss to one hundredth. This is why transmission uses very high voltage. At the consumer end the voltage must be reduced again to a safe level. Only a transformer can do this efficiently for AC. |
| 6 | Principle of Operation | • Primary winding connected to AC supply.<br>• Alternating flux Φ forms in the core.<br>• Flux links the secondary winding.<br>• Mutual induction produces EMF E2.<br>• Frequency stays the same; no moving parts.<br>**Formula/Key:** E1 = −N1·dΦ/dt,   E2 = −N2·dΦ/dt   →   E2/E1 = N2/N1 | Core-type transformer with primary N1, secondary N2, flux path Φ and laminated core. | The primary winding carries an alternating current that sets up an alternating flux in the iron core. Almost all the flux passes through the secondary winding and induces an EMF there by mutual induction. Because the same flux links both windings, the ratio of the induced voltages equals the ratio of the numbers of turns. The frequency is unchanged and there are no moving parts. |
| 7 | Types and Construction | • Core type: windings surround the core limbs.<br>• Shell type: core surrounds the windings.<br>• Core of thin silicon-steel laminations.<br>• Windings: insulated copper or aluminium.<br>• Oil or air cooling.<br>**Formula/Key:** Laminations (0.3–0.5 mm) cut eddy-current loss; silicon steel cuts hysteresis loss | Two cross-sections: core type with two wound limbs and shell type with a wound centre limb. | In the core-type transformer the windings are placed on two limbs of a rectangular core. In the shell type both windings are on the central limb of a three-limbed core, which gives a better magnetic path. The core is built from thin insulated laminations of silicon steel to reduce losses. Large transformers are immersed in oil for insulation and cooling. |
| 8 | Ideal Transformer and Turns Ratio | • No winding resistance, no leakage.<br>• Core has zero loss and infinite permeability.<br>• Voltage ratio equals turns ratio.<br>• Current ratio is the inverse.<br>• Input power equals output power.<br>**Formula/Key:** V2/V1 = N2/N1 = I1/I2 = K;   V1I1 = V2I2<br>K transformation ratio | Transformer symbol with V1, V2, I1, I2, N1, N2 and the ratio equations. | Start with the ideal transformer to understand the relations. The voltage ratio equals the turns ratio K. Since an ideal transformer has no loss, input volt-amperes equal output volt-amperes, so the current ratio is the inverse of the turns ratio. If K is greater than one the transformer steps the voltage up, otherwise it steps it down. |
| 9 | EMF Equation | • Flux Φ = Φm sin ωt in the core.<br>• Induced EMF = N·dΦ/dt, peak ωNΦm.<br>• RMS value = peak/√2.<br>• Substitute ω = 2πf.<br>• Φm = Bm × A.<br>**Formula/Key:** E = 4.44·f·N·Φm = 4.44·f·N·Bm·A  (V)<br>f Hz, N turns, Φm Wb, Bm T, A m² | Flux and induced EMF waveforms; EMF lags flux by 90°. Derivation steps listed beside. | The flux varies sinusoidally with peak value Φm. Differentiating gives an EMF of peak omega N Φm. Dividing by root two for the RMS value and putting omega equal to two pi f gives 4.44 f N Φm. The same expression holds for both primary and secondary windings, each with its own N. This equation is used to calculate the turns or flux for a given voltage. |
| 10 | Losses in a Transformer | • Iron loss: hysteresis plus eddy current.<br>• Constant at rated voltage and frequency.<br>• Copper loss: I²R in the windings.<br>• Varies as the square of the load current.<br>• No rotating parts: no friction loss.<br>**Formula/Key:** Total loss = Pi + x²·Pcu,fl   (x = load fraction) | Tree: transformer losses → iron (hysteresis, eddy) and copper (primary, secondary). | A transformer has two types of loss. The iron loss in the core depends on voltage and frequency, not on the load, so it is constant. The copper loss in the windings depends on the current and varies as the square of the load. These two determine the efficiency. Because there are no moving parts, a transformer is very efficient, typically above 95 per cent. |
| 11 | Efficiency | • Efficiency = output power / input power.<br>• Output = x·S·cos φ.<br>• Maximum efficiency: iron loss = copper loss.<br>• Load fraction at ηmax: x = √(Pi/Pcu,fl).<br>• Power transformers: designed for ηmax near full load.<br>**Formula/Key:** η = x·S·cos φ / (x·S·cos φ + Pi + x²·Pcu,fl);   ηmax at x = √(Pi/Pcu,fl) | Efficiency against load fraction with the maximum marked where iron loss equals copper loss. | Efficiency is the ratio of output to input power, and the input equals the output plus the losses. As the load increases, the output rises faster than the losses at first, then the copper loss takes over. The maximum occurs when the variable copper loss equals the constant iron loss. Distribution transformers that are lightly loaded most of the day are designed with a low iron loss. |
| 12 | Solved Example 1: Turns and Flux | • A 5 kVA, 2200/220 V, 50 Hz transformer has 60 secondary turns. Find N1, Φm, I1 and I2.<br>• 1. K = 220/2200 = 0.1.<br>• 2. N1 = N2/K = 600 turns.<br>• 3. Φm = E2/(4.44·f·N2) = 220/(4.44 × 50 × 60) = 16.5 mWb.<br>• 4. I1 = 5000/2200 = 2.27 A.<br>• 5. I2 = 5000/220 = 22.7 A.<br>**Formula/Key:** Answer: N1 = 600, Φm = 16.5 mWb, I1 = 2.27 A, I2 = 22.7 A | Left: steps; right: chain of four cards from K to turns, flux and currents. | The turns ratio is the voltage ratio, so the primary has ten times more turns. The flux follows from the EMF equation applied to the secondary. Rated currents are the kVA rating divided by the respective voltages. Check that the currents are in the inverse ratio of the voltages: 22.7 over 2.27 is ten. |
| 13 | Solved Example 2: Efficiency | • A 25 kVA transformer has iron loss 300 W and full-load copper loss 600 W. For pf 0.8 find η at full load, half load and maximum.<br>• 1. Full load: output 20 kW; losses 0.9 kW; η = 20/20.9 = 95.69 %.<br>• 2. Half load: output 10 kW; Pcu = 150 W; η = 10/10.45 = 95.69 %.<br>• 3. x for ηmax = √(300/600) = 0.707.<br>• 4. At x = 0.707: output 14.14 kW, losses 0.6 kW, η = 95.93 %.<br>**Formula/Key:** Answer: 95.69 % (full), 95.69 % (half), ηmax = 95.93 % at 70.7 % load | Left: steps; right: efficiency curve with the three points marked. | Efficiency is calculated from output over output plus losses. At full load the copper loss is its rated value, at half load it falls to one quarter. For the maximum efficiency we find the load fraction at which the copper loss equals the iron loss, which is the square root of the loss ratio. Notice that the efficiency curve is very flat near the maximum. |
