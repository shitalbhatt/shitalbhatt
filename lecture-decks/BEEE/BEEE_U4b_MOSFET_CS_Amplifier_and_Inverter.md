| 4 | Motivation: The Transistor of Modern Electronics | • Billions in a processor or phone.<br>• Very high input resistance; low power.<br>• Small, easy to integrate.<br>• Basic building block of logic gates.<br>**Formula/Key:** MOSFET dominates ICs: amplifiers, memories, processors, power switches | NMOS inverter circuit and its transfer curve (preview). | Almost every integrated circuit uses MOSFETs. | 5 | Structure of the n-Channel Enhancement MOSFET | • p-type substrate (body).<br>• Two n+ regions: source and drain.<br>• Thin SiO₂ insulating layer over the channel region.<br>• Metal gate on the oxide.<br>• No channel exists at VGS = 0.<br>**Formula/Key:** Terminals: Gate (G), Drain (D), Source (S), Body (B, tied to S);  L channel length, W width | Cross-section: p-substrate, n+ source and drain, oxide, gate, and the channel length L. | The n-channel MOSFET is built on a p-type substrate in which two heavily doped n regions form the source and drain. Between them, a thin insulating layer of silicon dioxide separates the metal gate from the substrate. Since the gate is insulated, no current flows into it. In the enhancement type there is no channel between source and drain until a gate voltage is applied. |
| 6 | Working of the MOSFET | • VGS < Vt: no channel; ID ≈ 0 (OFF).<br>• VGS > Vt: positive gate attracts electrons: n-channel forms.<br>• Small VDS: channel acts as a resistor (triode).<br>• Large VDS: channel pinches off near the drain.<br>• Beyond pinch-off ID is nearly constant (saturation).<br>**Formula/Key:** Channel induced when VGS > Vt;  pinch-off when VDS = VGS − Vt | Three cross-sections: no channel, uniform channel, and pinched-off channel. | At zero gate voltage the two n regions are separated by the p region, so no current flows. When the gate voltage exceeds the threshold voltage, the positive field attracts electrons to the surface and forms an n-type channel linking source and drain. For small drain voltage the channel behaves as a resistor. At larger drain voltage the channel narrows near the drain and the current levels off. |
| 7 | Output Characteristics | • ID against VDS for fixed VGS values.<br>• Triode region: ID rises with VDS (resistor-like).<br>• Saturation: ID nearly flat; used for amplification.<br>• Cut-off: VGS < Vt, ID = 0.<br>• Boundary: VDS = VGS − Vt.<br>**Formula/Key:** Triode: VDS < VGS − Vt;   saturation: VDS ≥ VGS − Vt;   cut-off: VGS < Vt | Family of ID–VDS curves for VGS = 2, 3, 4 V with the triode/saturation boundary parabola drawn dashed. | Each curve is for a fixed gate voltage. In the triode region the current increases roughly linearly at first, then the curve bends. At the boundary VDS equals VGS minus Vt the current reaches its saturation value. The saturation current grows with the square of VGS minus Vt, so the spacing of the curves increases. The MOSFET is OFF when VGS is below the threshold. |
| 8 | Square-Law Equations and Transfer Curve | • Saturation: ID depends only on VGS.<br>• Square-law relation with threshold Vt.<br>• kn depends on W/L and process.<br>• Transfer curve: ID against VGS.<br>**Formula/Key:** ID = ½·kn·(VGS − Vt)² (saturation);   kn = μn·Cox·W/L (mA/V²)<br>Triode: ID = kn[(VGS − Vt)VDS − VDS²/2] | Transfer curve ID–VGS: a parabola starting at Vt = 1 V with kn = 2 mA/V². | In saturation the drain current is half kn times the square of the gate overdrive voltage. The parameter kn depends on the width to length ratio of the channel, which the designer chooses, and on process constants. The transfer curve starts at the threshold voltage and rises as a parabola. In the triode region the current also depends on the drain voltage. |
| 9 | DC Load Line and Q-Point | • Drain circuit: VDD = ID·RD + VDS.<br>• Load line joins (VDD, 0) and (0, VDD/RD).<br>• Q-point: intersection with the VGS curve.<br>• Amplifier: Q in the saturation region.<br>• Switch: operate at the two ends.<br>**Formula/Key:** VDS = VDD − ID·RD;   example VDD = 12 V, RD = 1 kΩ, VGS = 3 V: Q = (8 V, 4 mA) | Output characteristics with the load line and Q-point at (8 V, 4 mA). | Apply Kirchhoff's voltage law around the drain circuit to get the load line. Its end points are the cut-off point at VDS equal to VDD, and the point where the drain current is VDD over RD. The intersection with the curve for the chosen gate voltage is the quiescent point. For an amplifier we place Q in the middle of the saturation region so the signal swing is symmetric. |
| 10 | Common-Source Amplifier | • Source is the common terminal.<br>• DC bias VGG sets the Q-point.<br>• Small input signal vi rides on VGG.<br>• Drain voltage varies inversely: vo opposite to vi.<br>• Gain larger than 1, phase 180°.<br>**Formula/Key:** Bias: ID = ½·kn(VGG − Vt)²;   VDS = VDD − ID·RD | CS amplifier circuit with VDD, RD, the input at the gate and output at the drain; input and inverted output waves. | In the common-source amplifier the input goes to the gate and the output is taken from the drain; the source is grounded. The DC bias voltage places the Q-point in saturation, and the small input signal changes the gate voltage a little. The drain current follows and the voltage drop across RD changes, so the drain voltage moves in the opposite direction. The output is an amplified, inverted copy of the input. |
| 11 | Small-Signal Gain | • Transconductance gm: change in ID per VGS.<br>• Output change: −gm·vgs·RD.<br>• Gain magnitude grows with RD and gm.<br>• Input resistance is very high.<br>• Output is 180° out of phase.<br>**Formula/Key:** gm = kn(VGS − Vt) = 2·ID/(VGS − Vt);   Av = −gm·RD<br>gm in mS (mA/V) | Four cards: gm formula, Av, phase shift and input resistance. | The transconductance is the slope of the transfer curve at the Q-point. A small gate voltage change gives a drain current change equal to gm times that voltage. The drain current change flowing through RD produces the output voltage change, so the voltage gain is minus gm times RD. The negative sign means that the output is inverted. |
| 12 | MOS Inverter | • NMOS with a drain resistor RD.<br>• Vin low (< Vt): MOSFET OFF → Vout = VDD (logic 1).<br>• Vin high: MOSFET ON, triode → Vout ≈ 0 (logic 0).<br>• Output is the logical NOT of the input.<br>• CMOS version replaces RD by a pMOS: no static power.<br>**Formula/Key:** Vin < Vt: Vout = VDD;   Vin = VDD: Vout = VDD − ID·RD ≈ VOL | NMOS inverter circuit and its voltage-transfer curve with logic levels. | The simplest inverter is an NMOS transistor with a load resistor. When the input is low the transistor is off, no current flows, so the output is pulled up to VDD. When the input is high the transistor conducts and pulls the output close to ground. So the circuit inverts logic levels. Modern chips use CMOS, where a pMOS transistor replaces the resistor and no static current flows. |
| 13 | Solved Example 1: Bias and Gain | • NMOS: Vt = 1 V, kn = 2 mA/V², VDD = 12 V, RD = 1 kΩ, VGS = 3 V. Find ID, VDS, region, gm and Av.<br>• 1. ID = ½ × 2 × (3 − 1)² = 4 mA.<br>• 2. VDS = 12 − 4 × 1 = 8 V.<br>• 3. VDS (8 V) > VGS − Vt (2 V): saturation, as assumed.<br>• 4. gm = kn(VGS − Vt) = 2 × 2 = 4 mS.<br>• 5. Av = −gm·RD = −4.<br>**Formula/Key:** Answer: ID = 4 mA, VDS = 8 V (saturation), gm = 4 mS, Av = −4 | Left: steps; right: chain of cards from ID to VDS, region check and gain. | Assume saturation and apply the square-law formula to find the drain current. Then use the drain loop equation to get VDS. Finally verify that the assumption was right: VDS is greater than the overdrive voltage. If it were not, we would have to redo the calculation with the triode equation. The gain is minus gm times RD, so the stage amplifies four times and inverts. |
| 14 | Solved Example 2: MOS Inverter | • VDD = 5 V, RD = 10 kΩ, Vt = 1 V, kn = 1 mA/V². Find Vout for Vin = 0 V and for Vin = 5 V.<br>• 1. Vin = 0 < Vt: MOSFET off, Vout = 5 V.<br>• 2. Vin = 5 V: assume triode: ID = 1·(4Vo − Vo²/2) mA.<br>• 3. Vo = 5 − 10·ID → 5Vo² − 41Vo + 5 = 0.<br>• 4. Vo = (41 − √1581)/10 = 0.124 V (< 4 V: triode ✓).<br>**Formula/Key:** Answer: Vout = 5 V for Vin = 0;  Vout ≈ 0.12 V for Vin = 5 V | Left: steps; right: voltage-transfer curve with the two operating points marked. | With zero input the MOSFET is off and the output rises to VDD. For a high input assume triode operation and write the drain current in terms of the output voltage. Equating it with the current through the load resistor gives a quadratic equation, of which only the small root is valid. The output is about 0.12 volts, a clean logic zero, and the triode assumption is confirmed because 0.12 is much less than 4. |
