| 3 | Recap: What You Already Know | • Capacitor charges as v = V(1 − e^(−t/RC)).<br>• Time constant τ = RC.<br>• Comparator output ±Vsat.<br>• Voltage divider: Vout = Vin·R2/(R1 + R2).<br>**Formula/Key:** Capacitor charging between two levels takes t = RC·ln[(Vf − V1)/(Vf − V2)] | Capacitor voltage zig-zag between 1/3 and 2/3 VCC (preview). | A capacitor charged through a resistor rises exponentially with time constant RC. Comparators switch their output when one input crosses the other. A voltage divider produces a fraction of the supply. The 555 timer combines these three ideas inside a single eight-pin package, and with two resistors and a capacitor it becomes an oscillator. |
| 4 | Motivation: Timers, Flashers and Regulators | • Flashing lights, buzzers, clock pulses.<br>• Pulse generation for digital circuits.<br>• Every power supply needs a regulator.<br>• Both are cheap, popular ICs.<br>**Formula/Key:** 555: pulse generator;   78XX: steady DC supply | Power-supply chain with a 78XX regulator (preview of the last slides). | The 555 timer is one of the most widely used ICs ever made. It produces timing pulses for flashers, alarms and clocks. The 78XX series is equally popular as the last stage of a DC power supply and gives a fixed voltage such as 5 volts for digital circuits. Both are examples of linear ICs designed to be used with a handful of external components. |
| 5 | 555 Timer: Internal Structure | • Voltage divider of three 5 kΩ resistors.<br>• Comparator 1 (threshold) compares with 2/3 VCC.<br>• Comparator 2 (trigger) compares with 1/3 VCC.<br>• SR flip-flop stores the state.<br>• Output stage and discharge transistor.<br>**Formula/Key:** Thresholds: upper = 2/3 VCC, lower = 1/3 VCC | Functional block diagram: divider, two comparators, flip-flop, output and discharge transistor. | Inside the 555 three equal resistors form a voltage divider that defines two thresholds at two thirds and one third of the supply. Two comparators check the external capacitor voltage against these thresholds and set or reset a flip-flop. The flip-flop controls the output pin and a transistor that discharges the timing capacitor. |
| 6 | 555 Pin Configuration | • Pin 1: ground; pin 8: VCC.<br>• Pin 2: trigger (set at 1/3 VCC).<br>• Pin 6: threshold (reset at 2/3 VCC).<br>• Pin 3: output; pin 4: reset (tie to VCC).<br>• Pin 7: discharge; pin 5: control voltage (0.01 µF to ground).<br>**Formula/Key:** Supply 4.5–16 V;  output current up to 200 mA | Eight-pin DIP outline with numbers and names of the pins. | Learn the eight pins: one is ground, eight is the supply. Two is the trigger input and six the threshold input, which are the two comparator inputs. Pin three is the output and pin seven discharges the capacitor. Pin four resets the timer when pulled low, so we normally tie it to the supply. Pin five sets the threshold voltage and is bypassed with a small capacitor. |
| 7 | Operation: Set and Reset | • Vc < 1/3 VCC: trigger comparator sets the flip-flop.<br>• Output HIGH; discharge transistor OFF; C charges.<br>• Vc > 2/3 VCC: threshold comparator resets it.<br>• Output LOW; discharge transistor ON; C discharges.<br>• Cycle repeats in astable mode.<br>**Formula/Key:** Set at 1/3 VCC;  reset at 2/3 VCC;  Vc swings between the two | Capacitor voltage between 1/3 VCC and 2/3 VCC with the set and reset conditions. | When the capacitor voltage is below one third of the supply the trigger comparator sets the flip-flop, the output goes high, and the discharge transistor turns off so the capacitor charges. When the voltage reaches two thirds of the supply the threshold comparator resets the flip-flop, the output goes low and the capacitor is discharged. In astable mode these two events alternate forever. |
| 8 | Astable Multivibrator Circuit | • External RA, RB and C set the timing.<br>• Pins 2 and 6 tied together to the capacitor.<br>• Pin 7 connects between RA and RB.<br>• No stable state: output toggles continuously.<br>• Also called a free-running oscillator.<br>**Formula/Key:** Charging path: VCC → RA → RB → C;   discharge path: C → RB → pin 7 | Astable 555 circuit with RA, RB, C, supply and output pin; pins labelled. | In the astable circuit the timing capacitor is charged through both resistors RA and RB from the supply. The discharge pin, connected between RA and RB, grounds that point through the internal transistor when the output is low, so the capacitor then discharges through RB alone. Because pins two and six are joined, the capacitor voltage triggers both comparators, and the circuit oscillates on its own. |
| 9 | Astable Waveforms | • C charges from 1/3 to 2/3 VCC through RA + RB.<br>• Output is HIGH during charging.<br>• C discharges from 2/3 to 1/3 VCC through RB.<br>• Output is LOW during discharging.<br>• Rectangular wave; duty cycle above 50 %.<br>**Formula/Key:** Charging τ = (RA + RB)C;   discharging τ = RB·C;   interval = τ·ln 2 = 0.693τ | Capacitor voltage and square-wave output over several cycles. | The capacitor voltage rises and falls between one third and two thirds of VCC. The time to charge from one third to two thirds is the time constant times natural log of two, which is 0.693 times the time constant. The charging time constant includes RA and RB, the discharge time constant only RB. So the output is high for longer than it is low. |
| 10 | Timing Equations | • High time depends on RA + RB.<br>• Low time depends only on RB.<br>• Frequency from the total period.<br>• Duty cycle always above 50 %.<br>• Use RA ≪ RB for a near-50 % duty cycle.<br>**Formula/Key:** tH = 0.693(RA + RB)C;  tL = 0.693RB·C;  f = 1.44/[(RA + 2RB)C];  D = (RA + RB)/(RA + 2RB) | Four cards with tH, tL, T and f with duty cycle. | Using the constants from the previous slide we get the high time as 0.693 times RA plus RB times C and the low time as 0.693 times RB times C. The period is their sum and the frequency is 1.44 divided by RA plus two RB times C. The duty cycle is the high time over the period, and since RA is positive it is always greater than fifty per cent. |
| 11 | 78XX Series Voltage Regulators | • Three-terminal fixed positive regulators.<br>• Last two digits give the output: 7805 = 5 V.<br>• Input must exceed output by about 2 V.<br>• Up to 1 A output with a heat sink.<br>• Built-in current limit and thermal shutdown.<br>**Formula/Key:** Vo = fixed (5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24 V);   Vin(min) ≈ Vo + 2 V;   79XX: negative series | 78XX regulator with input and output capacitors, GND, and a row of common device numbers. | The 78XX is a family of fixed-voltage regulators with only three pins: input, ground and output. The last two digits are the output voltage, so the 7805 gives 5 volts and the 7812 gives 12 volts. The input must be at least 2 volts above the output, otherwise regulation is lost. Small capacitors at the input and output improve stability, and the matching 79XX series gives negative voltages. |
| 12 | Regulator in a DC Power Supply | • Transformer lowers the mains voltage.<br>• Bridge rectifier gives pulsating DC.<br>• Capacitor filter smooths it.<br>• 78XX regulator gives fixed, ripple-free output.<br>• Regulator dissipates (Vin − Vo)·IL as heat.<br>**Formula/Key:** Preg = (Vin − Vo)·IL;   efficiency = Vo/Vin (linear regulator) | Four-block chain with a small waveform under each: AC, rectified, filtered, regulated. | A complete DC supply is the chain we have built over the last lectures: transformer, rectifier, filter, and regulator. The regulator keeps the output constant even when the mains voltage or the load changes. A linear regulator throws away the voltage difference as heat, so for large drops a heat sink is needed and the efficiency is the ratio of output to input voltage. |
| 13 | Solved Example 1: Astable Frequency | • A 555 astable has RA = 1 kΩ, RB = 10 kΩ, C = 0.1 µF. Find tH, tL, T, f and duty cycle.<br>• 1. tH = 0.693 × (1k + 10k) × 0.1 µF = 0.762 ms.<br>• 2. tL = 0.693 × 10k × 0.1 µF = 0.693 ms.<br>• 3. T = 1.455 ms;  f = 1/T = 687 Hz.<br>• 4. D = tH/T = 0.762/1.455 = 52.4 %.<br>**Formula/Key:** Answer: tH = 0.762 ms, tL = 0.693 ms, f = 687 Hz, duty = 52.4 % | Left: steps; right: four cards from the component values to timing, frequency and duty cycle. | Substitute the component values into the timing equations. Be careful with units: kilo-ohm times microfarad gives milliseconds. The two times add up to a period of 1.455 milliseconds, which is a frequency of 687 hertz. Because RA is much smaller than RB, the duty cycle is close to fifty per cent. |
| 14 | Solved Example 2: Design for 1 kHz | • Design an astable 555 for f = 1 kHz, duty cycle 60 %, C = 0.01 µF.<br>• 1. D = (RA + RB)/(RA + 2RB) = 0.6 → RB = 2RA.<br>• 2. f = 1.44/(RA + 2RB)C = 1.44/(5RA·C).<br>• 3. RA = 1.44/(5 × 1000 × 10⁻⁸) = 28.8 kΩ;  RB = 57.6 kΩ.<br>• 4. Substitute it into the frequency formula and solve for RA, then for RB. Finally choose the nearest standard resistor values and recompute the frequency and duty cycle to check that they are close to the specification. | 15 | Solved Example 3: 7805 Regulator | • A 7805 is supplied from 12 V DC and feeds a 100 Ω load. Find IL, power dissipated in the regulator and efficiency. Is the input voltage adequate?<br>• 1. The load current follows from Ohm's law. The regulator dissipates the voltage drop times the load current, which is 0.35 watts here, so a small heat sink may be needed. The efficiency of a linear regulator is simply the ratio of output to input voltage. |
